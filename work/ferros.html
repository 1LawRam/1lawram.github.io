Project Ferros · In development

A third hand
that pays attention.

Ferros watches where your soldering iron lands, waits for the joint to actually reach temperature, and feeds solder to that exact point. No foot pedal. No third hand you don't have.

01

Rest position

The sensor arch stands over the work area. The arm parks clear of the board and waits — nothing moves until you give it something to look at.

02

The arch watches

A depth camera and a thermal array look straight down from the apex. Midway up each of the three pillars, an RGB camera watches the same work area from its own angle — so a hand or a tall component can't hide the joint from every view at once.

03

Double-tap to designate

Tap the joint twice with the iron tip. A piezo disc under the fixture catches both impulses, the camera confirms the tip was on the board, and that pad becomes the target.

04

Solder, on the joint

The thermal array watches the pad. The moment its trace shows the melt signature, the feeder advances a metered length of wire — then retracts 2–3 mm to break the bridge.

The problem

Soldering needs three hands. You have two.

One hand holds the iron. One holds the board. The solder gets whatever's left.

The technique every tutorial teaches — iron heats the joint from one side, solder feeds into the joint from the opposite side — is exactly the part that gets sacrificed. Solder fed onto the tip instead of the joint loses its flux before it reaches the work, and you get the cold, grainy joint.

Helping-hands clamps and manual solder feeders exist, but they're static and human-triggered: you position them once and work around them, then press a pedal. For someone with the use of one arm, those workarounds collapse entirely. Ferros moves to you instead, and decides when to feed on its own.

The hard part

Molten solder lies to a thermal camera.

Infrared thermometry measures radiance and infers temperature from an assumed emissivity. Board material sits near 0.9 — easy. But the instant solder melts it becomes a shiny specular metal, and its emissivity collapses toward 0.05–0.15. The apparent temperature dips at the exact moment the real temperature is rising.

So a naive threshold — "feed at 190 °C" — mistimes every joint. The fix is to treat that artifact as the signal: the detector looks for the latent-heat plateau, the emissivity dip, and the adjacent board pixels rising smoothly past a calibrated band. A taped-on K-type thermocouple provides ground truth to calibrate it against.

Hardware

What's inside.

Depth
Luxonis OAK-D PoE

Stereo depth with on-device inference, looking down from the arch apex. Board-frame coordinates come from four fixture AprilTags, recomputed every frame.

Thermal
MLX90640, 55° FOV

32 × 24 long-wave IR, −40 to 300 °C, 16 Hz. Chosen over the AMG8833, which saturates at 80 °C and cannot see solder temperatures at all.

Actuation
6 × Feetech STS3215

30 kg·cm serial-bus servos with 4096-count magnetic encoders, daisy-chained on one wire and one 12 V rail.

Feeder
Pancake NEMA17

A silicone-faced drive wheel against a sprung idler — compliant grip instead of a knurled gear, so soft flux-cored wire isn't crushed.

Trigger
27 mm piezo disc

Sandwiched under the fixture plate. Two impulses 80–500 ms apart, gated on the camera seeing the tip near the board.

Structure
PETG-CF joints

Carbon-filled PETG at the shoulder carriage, rack, and brackets; standard PETG for the links. Everything printed and revised in CAD.

Where it stands

Build log.

  • DONE

    Engineering package

    An 18-page write-up covering architecture, the sensor trade study, feed mechanics, safety and risk analysis, a phased build plan with exit criteria, and a test plan built for ARKSEF.

  • DONE

    Sourcing and BOM

    Full parts list costed against a $300 cap, with a per-line walk-away price and a market-by-market sourcing map.

  • PHASE 1 · NEXT

    Feeder bench prototype

    Characterise commanded versus actual feed length, slip against idler tension, and jam modes. Exit criterion: ±0.5 mm over a 10 mm feed, 50 consecutive cycles jam-free.

  • PHASE 2

    Melt detection

    The emissivity study across SAC305 and Sn63, against thermocouple ground truth. Exit criterion: fire within ±0.4 s of confirmed liquidus on at least 18 of 20 trials, with zero false fires on 20 heat-without-melt trials.

  • PHASE 4

    Ferros Zero

    The full sensing chain with the nozzle positioned by hand — tap designates, thermal gates, feeder feeds and retracts. Deliberately a complete result on its own, so nothing downstream is load-bearing on the arm being finished.

  • FERROS 2

    Roadmap

    A second arm carrying the iron itself, turning the bench into a fully automated point-soldering cell at roughly a third the price of the cheapest machine that removes the human trigger.

Ferros is in the planning-to-build transition. Photos, source, and measured results go up here as each phase lands.
